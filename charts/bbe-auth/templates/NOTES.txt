Keycloak signs people in at https://auth.{{ .Values.global.bbe.domain }}, in the `bbe` realm.

The first admin is `{{ .Values.bbe.auth.admin.username }}`, who chooses a new password when first signing in with:
  kubectl get secret bbe-auth-admin -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} -o jsonpath='{.data.password}' | base64 -d

Admins manage people at https://auth.{{ .Values.global.bbe.domain }}/admin/bbe/console/, and `bbe configure {{ .Release.Name }}`
adds and removes them too. People in `admins` get into the apps behind oauth2-proxy, which manage the media, and
manage people in Keycloak. Those in `users` have an account, for apps that let everyone signed in through. Jellyfin and
Seerr keep their own logins.

Keycloak's own admin, for when nobody in the realm can sign in, is `bbe-admin` at
https://auth.{{ .Values.global.bbe.domain }}/admin/, with the password:
  kubectl get secret bbe-auth-keycloak -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} -o jsonpath='{.data.admin-password}' | base64 -d

Keycloak is being set up in the background, which takes a few minutes on a first install. To follow along:
  kubectl logs -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} -f job/bbe-auth-setup-{{ .Release.Revision }}
